ICACE 2026 • Under Review

Multi-Objective Performance Modeling of Recycled Aggregate Concrete: A Single-Trial Predictive Approach

Authors: Emad Ahmed, Rehnuma Raheem, Md. Asifur Rahman

Evaluates Linear Regression, SVM, Random Forest, and XGBoost on a Mendeley RAC mix dataset to jointly predict compressive strength, splitting tensile strength, manufacturing cost (Bangladesh PWD rates), and CO₂ emissions in a single pass. XGBoost achieved test R² values of 0.952 for compressive strength and 0.946 for splitting tensile strength, with R² > 0.997 for cost and emissions, while SHAP identified water-binder ratio, curing age, and cement content as primary drivers.

Research Fair 2023 • Poster 98

Fiber Reinforced Self-Compacting Concrete Using Fly Ash and Ground Granulated Blast Furnace Slag

Authors: Tahsin Mahmud, Labiba Mostafa, Emad Ahmed, Prof. Dr. G. M. Sadiqul Islam

Investigates self-compacting concrete mixes with 25% Fly Ash cement replacement, varying GGBFS (0–20%), and 0.1% volume polypropylene fibers under EFNARC 2005 and ACI 237R criteria. A 15% GGBFS blend achieved 39.4 MPa 28-day compressive strength and 4.8 MPa split tensile strength while maintaining low water absorption (< 5%), permeable voids (< 11%), and ductile post-cracking behavior.

M.Sc. Thesis Proposal • Ongoing

Machine Learning-Based Seismic Vulnerability Screening and Retrofit Decision Support for RC Buildings in Dhaka

Candidate: Emad Ahmed (24MCE719) • Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Basir Zisan (CUET)

Proposes a two-stage framework on 350 Rapid Visual Assessment (RVA) RC buildings in Dhaka using BNBC-2020 ETABS response spectrum targets (MIDR and DCR-failed walls/columns). Model 1 screens baseline safety (< 2.5% MIDR and zero DCR member failures), while Model 2 recommends retrofit strategies and estimates cost (Tk.) for non-compliant buildings, paired with stratified cross-validation and SHAP audits.

Video: MSCSeismicExplainer.mp4
Undergraduate Thesis (B.Sc.)

Natural Zeolite Concrete Under Hybrid Carbonation & Water Curing: Mechanical, Durability & Phase Behavior

Affiliation: Dept. of Civil Engineering, CUET

Evaluates concrete with 0–20% natural zeolite replacement under conventional water curing versus 24-hour early carbonation followed by water curing at 28 and 56 days. Slump decreased from 100 mm (plain cement) to 44 mm at 20% zeolite, while 10% replacement gave peak performance—reaching 38.42 MPa compressive strength (+15.0%), 3.62 MPa split tensile strength (+13.7%), and 1864 C RCPT charge passed (low permeability) at 56 days under carbonation + water curing. UPV correlated strongly with compressive strength (R² = 0.955–0.984), supported by TGA and FTIR characterization alongside water absorption and void tests.
